## 一、摘要原文

TRUST-Line recommends bus rapid transit (BRT) for the 7.101 km Finch East corridor from Yonge Street to Victoria Park Avenue, selected using a cost-independent, tiered first-stage model. Subway is excluded from the conservative base case because a credible nine-year delivery and depot strategy has not been demonstrated. We computationally compare BRT and light rail transit using capital effectiveness, relative ridership and travel-time savings. Explicit scenario inputs are CAD350 million versus CAD950 million, ridership indices of 1.0 versus 1.2, and one-way savings of 2.5 versus 3.5 minutes. BRT leads under equal and capital-efficiency priorities; service-benefit priorities favour LRT. Reference-project cost proportions and four milestones make assumptions transparent (Metrolinx, 2020; Toronto Transit Commission, 2026). We recommend assessing feasible corridor and utility reservations for possible future LRT conversion, which would require separate engineering, funding and approval. Key limitations are transferred costs, assumed demand and time savings, policy-dependent results, and delivery risks. The analysis does not establish verified total costs, guaranteed 2035 opening or seamless conversion.

### 第1页：TRUST-Line: Finch East BRT with Future LRT Options

We are S06. TRUST-Line makes two linked decisions: select the corridor independently of cost, then choose its technology. We recommend Finch East BRT, preserving feasible future rail options.

### 第2页：Part 1: five cost-independent indicators, all 24 assessed

Part One starts with all twenty-four City corridors. We retain five cost-independent need indicators: current speed, forecast load, current maximum load, competitiveness and a Census-based low-income concentration proxy. The City scores stay unchanged. Equity uses uniform within-tract allocation to a five-hundred-metre road-proxy catchment and is standardized against the sixteen common-baseline corridors.

The source-only correlation plot shows the relationships we actually examined. No pair reaches our exploratory absolute-correlation threshold of point seven, but that does not prove independence. Concept budgets and deletion tests address repeated weighting separately. Missing City cells use declared minimum and mean scenarios; suppressed Census contributions remain flagged. This is a transparent need-priority model with partial place and prosperity coverage, not a claim to measure every policy outcome.

### 第3页：A disputed input changed Jane 12 → Finch East 23

One source ambiguity changed our recommendation. Jane's reported subsection lengths appear inconsistent with road-proxy lengths, but we found no official correction. We did not swap lengths or alter standardized scores. Instead, we removed the disputed length-scaled current-ridership indicator and repeated the paired analysis.

Jane twelve's top-five support fell to about sixty-six point six percent. Finch East remained first or second in the six specified method and weight cases. Those cases were resampled ten thousand times with fixed selectors; they are six scenarios, not ten thousand independent data sets. This is why the source-quality check matters: the corridor choice responds to a disputed input rather than hiding its influence.

### 第4页：Select Finch East under the declared service/equity agenda

We therefore select corridor twenty-three under our declared service and equity agenda. All twenty-four receive contextual evaluation, while the eight conditional extensions retain prerequisite and bundle assessments. Their scores assume different networks, so we do not invent a pooled common-baseline rank.

The word robust needs a boundary. Finch East is top-five in one hundred percent of the original paired support, but only eighty-one point eight two percent under our broader illustrative block-weight prior. These are assumption-dependent preference frequencies, not statistical confidence. Stronger place-growth and employment evidence could change the decision. We accept and disclose that scope as we carry one fixed corridor into Part Two.

### 第5页：Screen first. Keep the cost assumptions visible.

Now we compare technologies on the fixed seven point one zero one kilometre corridor. Subway is excluded from our conservative base-case shortlist because a complete nine-year delivery programme and allocated depot/access strategy have not been demonstrated. This is a screening policy, not proof of physical impossibility.

We retain BRT and LRT with explicit cost points of three hundred and fifty million and nine hundred and fifty million dollars. The Eglinton West surface-option proportions allocate land, vehicles, systems and contingency within those totals. We remove the separately reported professional-services allowance as a disclosed proxy; it is not an audited consultant-only reconciliation. Nor does the allocation validate a complete project budget. Its low land share reflects airport acquisition assumptions. The purpose is to expose cost composition without adding the same components twice.

### 第6页：Four milestones to a conditional 2035 opening

Delivery uses four milestones, not a detailed construction schedule. Our assumed phases are one to two years for planning, one to two for property and utilities, three to four for core construction and one for testing. That is six to nine years.

The upper calendar starts in January twenty-twenty-seven and reaches operational readiness in December twenty-thirty-five. It has no extra delay buffer. Fleet, systems and depot access must fit inside these milestones. We do not claim a guaranteed opening or a proven four-to-six-year BRT programme. The recommendation carries explicit delivery conditions instead of treating the deadline as a slogan.

### 第7页：Compare benefits. Show where policy changes the answer.

Benefits are also declared assumptions. We use ridership indices of one and one point two, with one-way end-to-end savings of two and a half and three and a half minutes. TTC and City signal-priority pilots provide context; their selected-intersection maxima do not validate Finch East averages.

The existing model combines capital effectiveness, relative demand and time savings with ratio-to-best normalization. Equal weights give BRT point eight four nine and LRT point eight one four. Capital-efficiency priority also selects BRT; service-benefit priority selects LRT. Effectiveness contains demand, so we disclose the overlap and retain earlier definition and normalization sensitivities. Choosing BRT is a stated policy judgement, not a claim that every possible weighting selects it.

## 四、答辩问答原文

**Why are eight corridors outside the main ranking?** They assume predecessor projects already exist. We evaluate all 24 contextually and retain their full prerequisite/bundle assessments, but cannot invent comparable Base-0 outcomes for conditional extensions. This does not prove they are inferior or physically impossible.

**Did you correct the Jane data to help Finch East win?** No. The reported Jane subsection lengths remain unresolved. We retained official values and excluded the disputed length-scaled current-ridership input in a paired stress test. Corridor 12 then lost its all-scenario top-five status; 23 retained it in the original six cases.

**Is 100% scenario support statistical confidence?** No. The original selector resamples six declared cases. Broader block-weight preferences give Finch East 81.82% top-five inclusion, and other deletions can place it sixth. The recommendation is tied to the stated agenda.

**Why exclude Subway if the budget may accommodate it?** The conservative base-case policy requires a credible delivery/depot strategy inside the deadline. That has not been demonstrated for this option. We do not claim a proven duration lower bound, zero Wilson surplus or absolute physical impossibility.

**Is CAD350M a verified full capital estimate?** No. It is a declared scenario point. Reference proportions allocate components inside it, not on top. Price/scope transfer and consultant classification are limitations. The 2.10% budget share is arithmetic, not certified fiscal surplus.

**Do milestones guarantee 2035 opening?** No. The 6–9-year scenario reaches the deadline in the upper case without extra delay buffer. Fleet, systems and depot/access must fit inside it; they cannot become later uncounted phases.

**How much extra demand does LRT actually attract?** The 1.2 relative demand ratio is an explicit assumption, not an independent forecast or an inference from vehicle capacity. Equal-demand and other-demand scenarios remain in the supporting results.

**Does TTC evidence prove 2.5/3.5-minute savings?** No. TTC/City give selected-intersection maxima, not target averages. Fifteen signals and the end-to-end improvements are assumptions. Do not confuse a round-trip improvement, intersection delay and a passenger's average trip.

**Does E=R/C plus R double-count demand?** They share information. We disclose that dependence, retain the agreed formula and test policy priorities. The earlier inverse-cost and normalization views remain supporting sensitivity, rather than claiming independence or universal robustness.

**Why BRT if service-priority weights select LRT?** A decision needs a stated value basis. The unchanged equal baseline and capital-efficiency priority support BRT; service-benefit priority genuinely supports LRT. We choose the former agenda and show the switching boundary.

**Is future conversion to LRT seamless or free?** No. Option preservation means checking feasible clearances, coordinating utilities and retaining good records. Rail conversion would need separate engineering, approvals, vehicles, systems, maintenance/access, works and funding. Those future costs have not been estimated.

**How was AI used?** As disclosed: source review, data processing, code, modelling, analysis, figures and substantive writing. The team confirmed the applicable permission and retained responsibility for the decision. Do not represent these uses as grammar-only or unaided work.

**What remains to do?** Deliver the integrated script aloud with all four speakers, log real times and transitions, and review the final presentation and abstract before the team submits. Automated checks and word-rate estimates are not a completed human rehearsal.
